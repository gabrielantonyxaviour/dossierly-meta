# Muse model evaluation on Polish official letters

Reproducible measurement of the production Look pipeline (Spark grounding call, Spark reading call, SAM 3.1 box refinement, deterministic KPA deadline engine) across Meta's Muse models. Everything is run through the product's own code, not a re-implementation.

## Dataset (entirely fictional)

28 invented Polish official letters, rendered from templates modelled on the repo's sample letters (`scripts/samples/letters-html.mjs`). Every name, case number, address and date is made up, and each page carries a "PRZYKŁAD / SAMPLE" stamp and footer. No real person or authority document is used.

| type | letters | notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `wezwanie_braki` (KPA art. 64 § 2) | 12 | 2-4 requested documents; 8 general, 4 address-change related; periods 7, 14, 21, 30 days; three phrasings ("od dnia doręczenia", "od daty doręczenia", "w ciągu ... od dnia otrzymania") |
| `wezwanie_dokumenty` (art. 50 § 1) | 6 | enrolment-certificate requests; 3 with a period (7/14 days), 3 with a fixed "do dnia" date |
| `decision` | 6 | 3 granting, 3 refusing; 14-day appeal period |
| `appointment` | 4 | 3 fingerprint summonses, 1 card collection; date and time |

Two offices (Warsaw, Kraków), 28 distinct names, case numbers and letter dates. Each letter has a clean render (1240x1754) and two phone-photo variants (rotation 3-8 degrees, perspective jitter, desk background, cast shadow, uneven light, noise, blur, JPEG; 1500x2000). Ground truth (`eval/data/labels.json`): case number, letter date, type, requested documents, period, delivery date, the due date computed with `computeKpaDeadline`, and the deadline-sentence bounding box. The box is measured from the rendered DOM and transformed with the same homography as the photo.

Regenerate: `npx tsx eval/data/generate.ts && python3 -I eval/data/make_photos.py` (seeded, deterministic). The repo keeps the 28 clean PNGs; photo JPEGs are regenerated by the script.

## Method

- Harness: `MODEL_API_KEY=... npx tsx eval/run.ts` runs `readLook` (`src/lib/look/read.ts`) unchanged, with the vision role pointed at each model through the product's `MODEL_VISION_MODEL` override. The reading call runs at the configured reasoning effort; the grounding call keeps the production default (medium). Both use the same model.
- Deadline box: the item with role `deadline`. SAM refinement: that box goes to `sam-3.1` as a box prompt through `samSegment`, then the product's `parseSamMasks` and `outlineFor`, which falls back to the Spark box when the mask is unusable.
- Deadline correctness: the model's extracted period and start rule go to the product's deterministic engine with the true delivery date; the due date must equal the truth. Fixed-date letters count as correct when the extracted quote contains the true date.
- Letter type is derived from fields the product already extracts (appointment present, decision kind, art. 64 basis, otherwise asks).
- Requested-documents F1 matches each extracted ask (Polish + English text) against per-document keyword patterns (`eval/data/corpus.ts`); precision is the share of extracted asks matching a true document, recall the share of true documents matched.
- IoU is against the axis-aligned deadline-sentence box. Cost uses published prices: Spark $1.25/M in, $4.25/M out, $0.15/M cached; Wonder $4/M in, $20/M out (no cached rate published, billed as input); SAM $2.50 per 1,000 images. Latency is the wall time of the whole `readLook` (the two calls run in parallel) with retries included.
- Concurrency 4, up to 4 attempts with backoff on 5xx, 429 and network errors, hard budget stop below US$15.
- Raw per-image outputs: `eval/results/*.json`. Tables: `npx tsx eval/report.ts eval/results/*.json`.

## Takeaways

1. **Field extraction is saturated; grounding and latency separate the models.** Every config reads case number, letter date, type, requested documents and period at 98-100%, and every config's extracted period feeds the deterministic engine to the right due date at 98-100% (the only miss is a single `spark-1.2-low` photo). This is why the product lets the model only read the sentence and does the date arithmetic in code: once the period is read correctly, correctness is a property of the engine, not the model. These letters are clean, printed and in a fixed layout, so this is an upper bound, not a claim about arbitrary scans.
2. **Spark 1.3 is the right extraction and grounding model.** Mean Spark-box IoU is 0.54-0.55 on 1.3 against 0.26-0.30 on 1.1 and 1.2, and the share of boxes with IoU >= 0.5 is 60-67% against 25%. Extraction does not need the extra reasoning: `spark-1.3-high` costs 24% more and is about 5 s slower at p50 (23.6 s against 19.0 s), with no meaningful gain in extraction and a small, within-noise IoU gain. We keep 1.3 at low for Look.
3. **Wonder 1.0 does not pay for itself here.** Its grounding quality matches Spark 1.3 (IoU 0.51 against 0.54, SAM 0.62 against 0.63) and its extraction is also perfect, but it costs $0.109 per letter against $0.033 (3.3x) for the same result. Wonder is faster than 1.3 (13.6 s against 19.0 s p50) because it spends fewer reasoning tokens. We keep Wonder out of the default routing; it is the option if latency matters more than cost.
4. **Older Spark is cheaper and faster but ground poorly.** 1.1 and 1.2 answer in about 8 s but their boxes land on the right place less than 3% of the time at IoU >= 0.75 (1.3 reaches 13-15%). For text-only tasks (interpret, guard) where no box is needed, 1.2 at minimal is the cheap, fast choice. Minimal reasoning on 1.2 gave the same extraction but nearly useless grounding (IoU 0.09 on clean, against 0.15 for `spark-1.2-low`), so vision stays on 1.3 and 1.2 minimal is reserved for text roles.
5. **SAM refinement helps consistently.** Over the Spark box it lifts mean IoU by +0.09 to +0.11 on every config that grounds (for example 0.54 to 0.63 on 1.3-low), and the share at IoU >= 0.5 by 12-17 points (60% to 77% on 1.3-low; 25% to 42% on 1.1). It never fell back to the Spark box in this run (every SAM call returned a usable mask), at $0.0025 and about 2.5 s per image. SAM improves a roughly placed Spark box; it cannot rescue a box placed on the wrong lines, which explains why the gain is largest where the Spark box was already overlapping.
6. **Cost.** The full production path (two vision calls plus SAM) costs $0.027-$0.041 per letter on Spark and $0.109 on Wonder; about 8,300-8,900 input and 3,700-7,200 output tokens per letter.

## Limitations

- Fictional, template-generated letters: one layout family per type, no handwriting, stamps over text, multi-page letters or real scan artefacts. Extraction results are an upper bound for real letters.
- Saturated metrics (about 100%) cannot rank models on extraction. A harder set (degraded scans, rarer legal bases, German/English mixed letters) would be needed for that.
- Single run per cell, no repeats; a 1-letter difference is 1.8 points on 56 images. Differences under about 5 points are within noise.
- IoU is measured against an axis-aligned box. Rotated photos have larger boxes around slanted text, which makes IoU easier than on the upright clean pages (this is why the "photo" column reads higher than "clean"). Truth boxes cover the whole sentence including wrapped lines; the product's own highlight is a rotated band, which this metric does not score.
- `spark-1.2-minimal` has no photo runs; `spark-1.3` reasoning "low" and "high" apply to the reading call only (grounding stays at medium, as in production).
- Wonder's cached-input rate is not published; the cost is an upper bound. The document-F1 matcher is keyword-based and favours exact phrasing from the templates.
- Photo B variants and appointment-sentence boxes are in the dataset but not scored.
